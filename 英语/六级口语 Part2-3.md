## 一、个人陈述

### 1. Should AI replace human jobs? AI 是否应取代人类工作？

I think AI’s impact on human jobs is a double-edged sword, not a simple“replace or not” question. （AI 对人类工作的影响是把双刃剑）

On one hand, AI replaces tedious/high-risk jobs. AI excels at repetitive, high-risk tasks—like assembly line work in factories or data entry in offices.（一方面，AI 取代枯燥/高风险工作）

This replaces humans in tedious jobs, freeing us from physical strain or monotony. For example, many logistics companies now use AI-driven robots to sort packages, which is faster and reduces errors.On the other hand, AI can’t replace “human-centric” jobs（另一方面，AI 无法取代“以人为核心”的工作）AI can’t fully replace jobs requiring emotional intelligence, creativity, or critical thinking. Teachers, nurses, and artists rely on human connection or original ideas—AI might assist a nurse with patient data, but it can’t comfort a worried patient like a human can.What’s more, AI creates new jobs: AI trainers, data analysts, and robot maintenance engineers. So instead of fearing replacement, we should focus on upskilling to adapt to the AI era. （AI 创造新工作，提升技能是关键）

### 2. The impact of social media on interpersonal relationships 社交媒体对人际关系的影响

Social media has both strengthened and weakened interpersonal relationships.（社交媒体对人际关系的影响具有两面性，既有强化也有削弱作用）

Positively, it bridges distance. My cousin studying abroad and I video chat weekly via WeChat, sharing daily lives like we’re together. It also helps us stay in touch with old friends we might otherwise lose contact with.（从积极面看，社交媒体能跨越空间距离，维系远距离关系）

However, it brings problems too. Many people now prioritize “online interactions” over face-to-face ones. I’ve seen groups of friends sitting at a restaurant, all staring at their phones instead of talking. This reduces deep,meaningful communication—liking a post isn’t the same as having a heartfelt conversation. Worse, social media often shows “perfect” versions of others’lives, making some people feel lonely or inadequate.（从消极面看，社交媒体易让人重“线上互动”轻“线下沟通”，

削弱真实连接）

So we need to use social media wisely: let it be a tool to enhance relationships,not replace real connections.（综上，关键在于合理使用，让社交媒体成为关系的“强化剂”而非“替代品”）

### 3. How to balance economic development and environmental protection? 如何平衡经济发展与环境保护？

Balancing economic development and environmental protection needs joint efforts from governments, enterprises, and individuals.（平衡二者需政府、企业、个人三方协同，缺一不可）

First, governments should make strict policies & provide guidance. For example,setting high environmental standards for factories—punishing those that discharge waste illegally, and rewarding those that use clean energy like solar or wind power. China’s “Green Development” strategy is a good example: it promotes eco-friendly industries while limiting high-pollution ones.（第一，政府需发挥政策引导与监管作用，设定环保标准）

Second, enterprises should take social responsibility. They can invest in green technology—like developing recyclable materials instead of single-use plastics,or upgrading production lines to reduce carbon emissions.（第二，企业需承担环保责任，通过技术升级减少污染）

Third, individuals can contribute too. Small actions matter: using public transport instead of driving, bringing reusable bags when shopping, and saving water and electricity.（第三，个人需从日常小事入手，践行环保理念）

Economic development doesn’t have to come at the cost of the environment—with smart planning, we can have both a strong economy and a healthy planet.（最终结论：通过科学规划，经济发展与环境保护可实现“双赢”）

### 4. Should plastic packaging be banned? 是否应禁止塑料包装？

I think plastic packaging should be restricted but not completely banned—we need a balanced approach.（对塑料包装不应“一刀切”禁止，而需采取“限制+优化”的平衡方案）

Plastic packaging has drawbacks: it’s non-biodegradable, polluting oceans and land. Every year, millions of plastic bottles end up in the sea, harming marine life like turtles and fish.（塑料包装的核心问题是不可降解，易造成生态污染）

However, it’s also convenient and cheap, especially for food safety. It keeps food fresh and prevents contamination, which is hard to replace in some cases,like packaging for takeout or medicine.（但塑料包装具备不可替代性，尤其在保障食品安全、降低成本方面）

So instead of a total ban, we can take three key measures. First, promote alternatives: Use paper bags, glass containers, or biodegradable plastics made from corn starch. Second, enforce recycling: Governments can set up more recycling bins and require enterprises to label plastic products clearly for recycling. Third, raise public awareness: Teach people to reduce plastic use, like bringing their own cups to coffee shops.（因此，替代“全面禁止”的是三项具体措施）

This way, we cut plastic pollution without losing the convenience plastic brings.（通过这些措施，可在减少污染的同时，保留塑料包装的便利性）

### 5. Is online education as effective as traditional education?线上教育是否与传统教育同样有效？

Online education is effective in some ways but not as comprehensive as traditional education.（线上教育在特定场景有效，但整体不如传统教育全面，需分维度分析）

Its strengths are flexibility and accessibility. Students in remote areas can take courses from top universities like Harvard or Tsinghua online, and working people can study after work without commuting. During the COVID-19 pandemic, online classes let us keep learning when schools were closed.（线上教育的核心优势是灵活性与可及性，打破时空限制）

However, it has two obvious limitations. For one, it lacks face-to-face interaction: Teachers can’t easily notice if students are confused, and students miss out on group discussions that spark ideas. For another, it requires strong self-discipline: Without a classroom environment, some students get distracted by phones or games and fall behind.（但线上教育存在两大局限，影响学习效果）

Traditional education, by contrast, offers real-time communication, hands-on activities (like science experiments), and a sense of community.（相比之下，传统教育在互动、实践与集体感上更具优势）

So online education is a great supplement to traditional education, but it can’t fully replace it—especially for younger students or subjects that need practical practice.（最终定位：线上教育是传统教育的“优质补充”，而非“完全替代品”）

### 6. Should college students start their own businesses? 大学生是否应创业？

College students starting their own businesses is worth encouraging but not for everyone—it depends on personal conditions.（大学生创业值得鼓励，但需因人而异，不能盲目跟风）

The benefits are clear. First, it teaches practical skills like problem-solving,teamwork, and time management, which textbooks can’t fully provide. Second,some successful examples, like Zhang Yiming (founder of ByteDance) who started exploring business ideas in college, show it can lead to great achievements. Third, it helps students turn creative ideas into real value—like developing a new app to solve campus life problems.（大学生创业的积极意义主要体现在三方面）

However, there are obvious risks. College students often lack experience,money, and social resources: many start-ups fail because they can’t handle market challenges or manage finances well. Also, starting a business takes a lot of time, which might affect their studies—grades and academic foundation are still important for their future.（但大学生创业也面临两大核心风险，需理性看待）

So students who want to start a business should first assess themselves. Do they have a solid business plan, support from mentors, and enough time? If yes, go for it; if not, it’s better to gain experience first after graduation.（因此，想创业的学生需先做好自我评估，再做决定）

### 7. How to improve students' participation in extracurricular activities? 如何提高学生课外活动参与度？

Improving students' participation in extracurricular activities needs to focus on three core directions: making activities more attractive, accessible, and meaningful.（提升参与度需从“吸引力、易参与性、意义感”三个核心方向入手）

First, diversify activities to match different interests. For example, besides sports and music clubs, add clubs for robotics, volunteer work, or cultural exploration—so students who like science or helping others can also find something they love.（第一，丰富活动类型，覆盖更多学生兴趣点）

Second, reduce barriers to participation. Some students don’t join because activities conflict with classes or cost money. Schools can schedule activities after class hours, provide free materials, or offer transportation for off-campus activities like volunteer trips to rural areas.（第二，消除参与障碍，降低学生参与门槛）

Third, highlight the value of activities. Let students know that extracurricular activities help them make friends, develop skills (like leadership from organizing events), and even boost college applications or job resumes.Teachers can also join activities sometimes to encourage students—if a math teacher plays basketball with the sports club, students might be more willing to join.（第三，明确活动价值，让学生感知参与的好处）

### 8. Is a vegetarian diet healthy? 素食是否健康？

A vegetarian diet can be healthy if planned well, but it also has risks if not balanced.（素食的健康与否，关键在于“饮食规划是否合理”，而非“素食本身”）

The advantages are clear. Vegetarian diets are rich in fiber, vitamins, and minerals from fruits, vegetables, and whole grains, which help lower the risk of heart disease and diabetes. Many vegetarians also consume less saturated fat(found in meat), which is good for overall health.（合理素食的健康益处主要体现在两方面）

However, a poorly planned vegetarian diet can lead to nutrient deficiencies.Meat is a main source of protein, iron, and vitamin B12—if vegetarians don’t replace these, they might feel tired (from low iron) or have weak immunity(from low protein).（但不合理的素食易导致三大营养素缺失，影响健康）

So to keep a healthy vegetarian diet, people need to take three key steps. First,choose the right foods: eat beans, tofu, or lentils for protein, spinach or fortified cereals for iron. Second, take vitamin B12 supplements if needed. Third, consult a nutritionist to make a balanced meal plan.（因此，保持健康素食需做好三项关键措施）

In short, being vegetarian isn’t automatically healthy—it’s about how you structure your diet.（总结：素食不代表“天然健康”，科学的饮食结构才是核心）

### 9. How to deal with culture shock when studying overseas?留学时如何应对文化冲击？

Dealing with culture shock when studying overseas requires three key strategies:keeping an open mind, learning actively, and seeking support.（应对留学文化冲击，需掌握“开放心态、主动学习、寻求支持”三大策略）

First, keep an open attitude. Culture shock often comes from differences in daily life—like eating habits or social norms. Instead of judging these differences as“weird,” try to understand them. For example, if people in your host country greet others with a kiss on the cheek (instead of a handshake), don’t feel uncomfortable—try to adapt, and you’ll feel more accepted.（第一，保持开放心态，避免用固有认知评判差异）

Second, learn about the local culture actively. Read books, watch local movies before arriving; after arriving, take language classes — learning the local language not only helps with communication but also lets you connect with locals on a deeper level.（第二，主动学习当地文化，从语言到习俗逐步融入）

Third, don’t be afraid to seek help. Talk to other international students—they’re going through the same thing, so you can share feelings and tips. You can also join campus groups for international students or talk to a counselor if you feel stressed.（第三，主动寻求支持，借助外部力量缓解冲击）

Remember, culture shock is normal—it takes time, but with effort, you’ll eventually feel at home.（最后需明确：文化冲击是正常现象，只要积极应对，终将适应）

### 10. Should traditional festivals be commercialized? 传统节日是否应商业化？

Traditional festivals should be commercialized moderately, not excessively—commercialization can promote them but might also erase their true meaning.（传统节日可“适度商业化”，但需警惕“过度”，避免丢失文化本质）

The good side of commercialization is obvious. It makes festivals more visible:For example, during the Mid-Autumn Festival, mooncake brands launch creative flavors and beautiful packaging, which attracts young people who might otherwise forget the festival. It also spreads the festival’s culture:Commercial activities like dragon boat races during the Dragon Boat Festival draw more participants and spectators, spreading the festival’s history.（适度商业化对节日的积极作用主要有两点）

However, over-commercialization is a serious problem. Some businesses focus only on making money—selling overpriced festival gifts or creating “fake traditions” just for profit. This makes festivals feel like “shopping sprees”instead of times for family reunion or cultural reflection, weakening their core values.（但过度商业化会带来两大负面影响）

So the key is to balance commercialization and cultural protection.Governments can regulate commercial activities and support culture-focused events; media can promote the festival’s cultural meaning, reminding people of its true value.（因此，核心在于“平衡”，让商业化服务于文化传承）

In short, commercialization itself isn’t bad—it’s a tool to promote traditional festivals. But we must never let it replace the cultural and emotional core of festivals.（总结：商业化是“工具”而非“目标”，需守住节日的文化与情感内核）

## 二、两人讨论

### 1. Your university plans to ban smartphones in classrooms.

**Student A:** Hey, did you hear our university might ban smartphones in classrooms? What do you think about that?

**Student B:** Honestly, I think it’s a good idea—smartphones are a big distraction in class. Last week, I sat next to a classmate who was scrolling through social media the whole time during the math lecture. He even missed the teacher’s important explanation about the exam topics! Do you not agree?

**Student A:** I get your point, but I’m a bit worried about the downsides. What if there’s an emergency? Like, if a family member needs to contact us urgently during class. Without phones, we might miss the call. Also, some students use phones to take notes—typing is faster than writing for them.

**Student B:** Oh, those are valid concerns. But maybe the university can set rules to fix that? Like, letting students keep phones on "silent mode" instead of completely banning them — so they can take emergency calls outside the classroom. And for note-taking, the teacher can provide slides after class, so students don’t have to rely on phones. Plus, when phones are banned, most students will focus more—have you noticed how many people check their phones every 10 minutes in class?

**Student A:** Yeah, that’s true. I’ve even caught myself checking messages without realizing it. Maybe a partial ban (like no using phones during lectures) is better than a total one. It balances focus and convenience. Do you think the university will consider that?

**Student B:** I hope so. If they just ban phones completely, some students might get upset. But if they explain the reason (to improve learning) and make flexible rules, most people will understand. Also, teachers can remind students to put phones away instead of just enforcing a strict ban—it feels more respectful.

**Student A:** Exactly! So the key is to make the policy reasonable, not too strict.That way, it helps us study better without causing too much trouble.

### 2. Should college students take part-time jobs? Why or why not?

**Student A:** Do you think college students should take part-time jobs? My roommate has been working at a coffee shop for two months, and she says it’s great, but I’m not sure if I should try it.

**Student B:** I think it’s a good idea if you can balance work and study. My cousin worked as a tutor part-time last year — she earned money to pay for her textbooks, and she also learned how to communicate with kids. It’s not just about money; it’s about gaining real-world experience. Why are you hesitant?

**Student A:** I’m worried it will take too much time. My courses this semester are really hard—if I work 20 hours a week, I might not have time to study or finish homework. I’ve heard some students failed exams because they focused too much on part-time jobs.

**Student B:** That’s a big risk! You’re right—study should be the first priority. So if you take a part-time job, you need to choose one with flexible hours, like working 8-10 hours a week on weekends. And avoid jobs that are too tiring, like night shifts—they’ll make you sleepy in class. Also, pick a job related to your major if possible. For example, if you’re a journalism student, working at a local newspaper as an intern is better than a coffee shop—it helps with your future career.

**Student A:** Oh, that’s a good tip! I’m a marketing student, so maybe I can look for internships at small marketing companies. That way, I can learn skills while earning money. But how do I find time to balance everything?

**Student B:** Make a schedule! Write down your class times, study hours, and work hours. For example, study in the morning, go to class in the afternoon,and work 4 hours on Saturday. And don’t forget to leave some free time to rest—burnout is worse than not having a part-time job. If you find work is affecting your grades, just quit temporarily.

**Student A:** That makes sense. So part-time jobs are good if you plan carefully,but bad if you neglect study. I think I’ll try to find a short internship this semester—start with a few hours a week to see how it goes.

### 3. Some people believe that space exploration is a waste of money, while others think it's crucial for human development. Discuss both views.

**Student A:** Have you seen the news about the new space mission? Some people say it’s a waste of money—what do you think? I mean, there are still people living in poverty on Earth; shouldn’t we use that money to help them first?

**Student B:** I get why they think that—space exploration is really expensive. For example, NASA’s Mars missions cost billions of dollars. That money could build schools or hospitals in poor areas. But I also think space exploration is crucial for human development. Remember how we developed satellite technology from space research? Now we use satellites for GPS, weather forecasting, and even internet access in remote areas. Those technologies help millions of people every day.

**Student A:** Oh, right! I never thought about that. But what about missions to Mars or the moon? Do they really help us now? It seems like a long-term goal,not something that fixes current problems.

**Student B:** That’s true, but long-term goals matter too. For example, if Earth’s resources run out someday (like oil or clean water), we might need to live on other planets. Space exploration helps us learn if that’s possible. Also, it pushes science forward—researching space leads to new inventions, like better medical devices or stronger materials. And it inspires people, especially kids, to study science and engineering.

**Student A:** I see your point. But maybe we should balance both? Use some money for space exploration, but not too much—make sure we also solve Earth’s problems like poverty and climate change. Do you think that’s possible?

**Student B:** Definitely! Governments can allocate budgets wisely. For example,spend 5% of the national budget on space research, and the rest on education,healthcare, and environmental protection. That way, we invest in the future without ignoring the present. Space exploration isn’t a waste if it benefits humanity in the long run.

### 4. How does consumerism affect young people's values?

**Student A:** I’ve noticed that many young people around me are obsessed with buying expensive brands—like designer bags or limited-edition sneakers. Do you think consumerism is changing their values?

**Student B:** Absolutely. Consumerism makes some young people equate"happiness" with "buying things." My cousin is always saving up to buy the latest iPhone—she says if she doesn’t have it, she feels "left out" by her friends.It’s like they judge themselves and others based on what they own, not who they are. That’s a bad change in values, right?

**Student A:** Yeah, it is. And social media makes it worse—everyone posts photos of their new purchases, so others feel pressured to keep up. I’ve even heard some students take part-time jobs just to buy luxury items, instead of saving money for study or future goals. But is there any positive effect?

**Student B:** Hmm, maybe a small one—consumerism can encourage young people to work hard to earn money. For example, if someone wants to buy a nice laptop for their studies, they might work part-time to get it, which teaches them responsibility. But the problem is when it’s excessive—when they buy things they don’t need just to show off.

**Student A:** Exactly! And it can make young people forget about more important values, like kindness or hard work. Instead of focusing on their grades or helping others, they focus on "what to buy next." How do you think we can fix this?

**Student B:** I think education is key. Schools can teach students about rational consumption—how to distinguish "needs" from "wants." Parents can also set a good example — instead of buying expensive toys for kids, take them to volunteer or travel, which teaches them that happiness comes from experiences,not things. And we can remind each other: it’s okay to not have the latest trends.

### 5. Is online education as effective as traditional education?

**Student A:** Hey, I’ve been thinking about our last class—do you really think online education is as effective as traditional in-person classes? I took an online programming course last month, and I struggled so much with the practical exercises.

**Student B:** I get your frustration! Online learning definitely has flaws, especially for hands-on subjects. Like, you can’t ask the teacher to walk you through coding errors in real time, right? But for theoretical courses—like literature or history—it’s actually pretty effective. My cousin studied world history online and aced her exam because she could rewatch lectures whenever she forgot key points.

**Student A:** That’s a good point about flexibility. But don’t you miss the in-class interactions? In my traditional philosophy class, we have heated debates every week, and hearing my classmates’ ideas helps me see different perspectives.Online discussions feel so slow—you post a comment and wait hours for a reply.

**Student B:** Oh, I totally agree! The lack of real-time interaction is a big downside.My little sister is in high school, and she hates online math classes because she can’t raise her hand to ask a question immediately. But here’s the thing—online education is a lifesaver for people with busy schedules. My aunt is a working mom who’s getting her master’s online; she studies after her kid goes to bed,which she couldn’t do with traditional classes.

**Student A:** Fair enough! Flexibility is a huge plus. But what about self-discipline?I know so many people who skip online lectures because there’s no teacher watching. In traditional classes, being in a classroom with others keeps me accountable—I don’t want to be the only one not taking notes.

**Student B:** That’s a valid concern! Self-discipline is key for online learning. But traditional classes have their own issues too—like fixed schedules. If you get sick or have a family emergency, you miss the class entirely. Online classes let you watch recordings later. I think it’s not about which is “more effective,” but which fits your needs. For self-disciplined people who need flexibility, online works; for those who need structure and interaction, traditional is better.

**Student A:** Exactly! Maybe the best solution is hybrid learning—like having in-person labs for science courses and online lectures for theory. That way, we get the best of both worlds.

**Student B:** I couldn’t agree more! Hybrid classes solve so many problems. My university tried it last semester, and most students loved it. It’s definitely the future of education, I think.

### 6. Should college students start their own businesses?

**Student A:** Did you hear about Mark, our senior? He dropped out to start a tech startup, and now his company is struggling. Do you think college students should take that kind of risk?

**Student B:** Oh, that’s sad. I don’t think dropping out is a good idea—college gives us a safety net. But starting a business while studying? That can be great if you plan it right. My friend Lisa runs a small online store selling handmade stationery—she only works on weekends, so it doesn’t affect her grades. She even earned enough to pay for her study abroad trip!

**Student A:** That sounds awesome, but where do you get the money and experience? Most of us don’t have savings, and banks won’t lend to students without work history.

**Student B:** Good question! Many schools offer support—my university has a“Student Entrepreneurship Fund” that gives small grants to good ideas. There are also competitions, like the National College Startup Contest. Last year, a team won $15,000 for their app that helps students find part-time jobs. They used that money to develop the app, and now it’s used by five local universities.

**Student A:** That’s helpful, but what about time? Running a business takes so much energy. Lisa might have it under control, but I know someone who skipped exams to attend business meetings. Isn’t that putting the cart before the horse?

**Student B:** You’re right—priorities matter. Lisa delegates tasks to her team: one classmate handles customer service, another does marketing. She also talks to her professors—her business professor let her extend a paper deadline because she was at a startup conference. But if you can’t balance work and study, it’s better to wait.

**Student A:** So it’s not a “yes or no” answer—it depends on preparation. You need a solid idea, support, and time management skills. If you don’t have those, it’s smarter to wait until after graduation.

**Student B:** Exactly! College is a great time to test small ideas and learn from mistakes, but you shouldn’t risk your education. If you can balance both, go for it—if not, there’s no rush. Entrepreneurship is a journey, not a race.

**Student A:** I totally agree. I used to think startups were only for “risk-takers,”but now I see it’s about smart planning. Maybe if I have a good idea someday,I’ll try it—after making sure my grades are stable first!

**Student B:** That’s the right mindset. Start small, stay focused, and don’t be afraid to ask for help. Who knows—your idea might be the next big thing for college students!
